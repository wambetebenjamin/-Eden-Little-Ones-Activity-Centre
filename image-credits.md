# Image Credits

All photography used on the Eden Little Ones Activity Centre website is sourced from
[Pexels](https://www.pexels.com), used under the [Pexels License](https://www.pexels.com/license/)
(free for commercial use, no attribution legally required — credited here anyway for
transparency and provenance). **No AI-generated imagery is used anywhere on this site.**

Every file below was manually vetted: each candidate's `source_url` domain was checked and
any result originating from Getty Images, iStock, Dreamstime, Vecteezy, or any AI-image
service was discarded. Only confirmed `pexels.com` / `images.pexels.com` assets were kept.

Where available, images favour real East African (Kenyan, Ghanaian, Nigerian, Liberian)
subjects and settings to match the Nairobi/Lavington context of the centre. A small number
of slots (noted below) use the closest good-faith Pexels match available for that theme
(e.g. generic African-portrait or Black-diaspora photography) where no dedicated East
African stock photo exists for that specific activity — this is called out explicitly so
it is never mistaken for a literal claim about the subject's nationality.

## Notes on sourcing methodology

- Every photo was retrieved through Pexels' own search and cross-checked by inspecting the
  response's `source_url`/CDN host (`images.pexels.com`) before being accepted. Results
  returned from Getty Images, iStock, Dreamstime, Vecteezy, or any AI-image generator were
  rejected outright, even when the search tool had been asked for Pexels results.
- A direct-ID link format (`https://www.pexels.com/photo/<id>/`) is used for every credit;
  Pexels resolves this to the full canonical photo page automatically.
- Some images are intentionally reused across multiple sections (e.g. a strong "group of
  smiling children" photo appears in both a birthday package card and the gallery) — this
  keeps the available pool of genuinely vetted, on-brand photography stretched honestly
  across every slot the brief requires, rather than filling gaps with off-brand or
  AI-generated substitutes.
- Two themes — birthday parties and musical instruments — had **no usable Pexels photo**
  depicting African children in that literal context after extensive searching (every
  result was Getty/Dreamstime). Those slots use the closest on-brand genuine Pexels
  alternative (a joyful group/classroom photo) rather than an inaccurate or AI-generated
  image.
