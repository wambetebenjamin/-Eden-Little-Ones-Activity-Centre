Parents often ask our facilitators: "How do I raise a creative child?" The
truth is every child is naturally creative — our job as parents and educators
is to protect that spark rather than accidentally dim it.

## Give open-ended materials

Toys and activities with one "correct" outcome leave less room for imagination
than open-ended materials like blocks, paint, clay and loose parts. Our **Arts
and Crafts** and **Science Lab** sessions are deliberately open-ended for this
reason.

## Ask "what if" questions

Instead of correcting a child's drawing of a purple elephant, ask "what if
elephants really were purple — where would they live?" Curiosity-led questions
keep imagination alive.

## Make space for boredom

Constantly scheduled children rarely get the quiet, unstructured time that
sparks original ideas. Build in unstructured play time at home between
activities.

## Celebrate the process, not just the result

Praise the effort and ideas behind a piece of art or a story, not just whether
it "looks right." This builds confidence to keep experimenting.
